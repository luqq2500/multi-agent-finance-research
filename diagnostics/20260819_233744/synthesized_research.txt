### Direct Answer
Comparing Tesla and General Motors (GM) capex spending practices reveals distinct magnitude, allocation, and financial scaffolding structures tailored to their respective operations. Tesla reported total capital expenditures (purchases of property, plant, and equipment) of **$7.3 billion for FY2023** and **$6.8 billion for FY2024** [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828024043486/tsla-20240930.htm]. In contrast, General Motors guided its anticipated full-year 2024 capital spending to be between **$10.5 billion and $11.5 billion** (inclusive of battery cell manufacturing joint ventures) [src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-releases-2024-third-quarter-results-and-updates-full-year].

### Supporting Evidence

#### Capital Expenditure Magnitudes and Tracking
- **Tesla:** FY2023 capex was $7.3 billion, decreasing to $6.8 billion in FY2024 [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828024043486/tsla-20240930.htm]. Quarterly tracking during 2024 showed $2.272 billion in Q2, $3.513 billion in Q3, and $2.780 billion in Q4 [src: https://ir.tesla.com/_flysystem/s3/sec/000162828025035738/tsla-20250723-gen.pdf]. 
- **General Motors:** Guided anticipated full-year 2024 capital spending to **$10.5 billion to $11.5 billion** (inclusive of battery cell manufacturing joint ventures) [src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-releases-2024-third-quarter-results-and-updates-full-year]. This range was maintained consistently across Q1, Q3, and full-year earnings disclosures throughout 2024 [src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-releases-2024-first-quarter-results-and-raises-full-year].

#### Strategic Investments and Competitive Advantage
- **Tesla:** Capital deployments support long-term scaling in vehicle manufacturing, energy storage (Megapack), and autonomy/AI initiatives [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828024043486/tsla-20240930.htm, src: https://ir.tesla.com/_flysystem/s3/sec/000162828025035738/tsla-20250723-gen.pdf].
- **General Motors:** Major allocations target the electrification transition, scaling EV production, developing battery cell manufacturing joint ventures, and a strategic **$4 billion investment in U.S. manufacturing plants** to support new product introductions and plant flexibility [src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-invest-4-billion-its-us-manufacturing-plants, src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-releases-2024-third-quarter-results-and-updates-full-year]. Management connects these investments to a profitable portfolio of EVs, securing raw material supply chains via joint ventures, and responding to changing consumer preferences [src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-invest-4-billion-its-us-manufacturing-plants].

#### Balance Sheet Fortress and Cash Flow Metrics
- **Tesla:** Total cash, cash equivalents, and investments reached **$36.563 billion** by Q4 2024 [src: https://ir.tesla.com/_flysystem/s3/sec/000162828025035738/tsla-20250723-gen.pdf]. 
- **General Motors:** Backed its capital strategy with projected FY2024 automotive operating cash flow of **$22.0 billion to $24.0 billion** and adjusted automotive free cash flow projected at **$12.5 billion to $13.5 billion** [src: https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-releases-2024-third-quarter-results-and-updates-full-year].

### Interpretation and What Would Close the Gaps
- **Tesla:** The reduction in capital expenditures from $7.3 billion in FY2023 to $6.8 billion in FY2024 suggests a disciplined approach to asset deployment while maintaining high absolute levels of investment. Its massive liquidity cushion ($36.563 billion exiting Q4 2024) indicates management preserves strong balance sheet strength to fund heavy R&D and capital investments independently.
- **General Motors:** GM utilizes a disciplined approach to capital allocation, funding multi-billion-dollar investments in next-generation EV architecture, battery cell joint ventures, and U.S. manufacturing footprint modifications directly from robust automotive operating cash flows. Management aims to self-fund the structural transformation toward electrification without compromising balance sheet resilience.
