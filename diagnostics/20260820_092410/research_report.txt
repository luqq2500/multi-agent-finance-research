## Executive Summary
Tesla, Microsoft, Alphabet (Google), Meta Platforms, and NVIDIA display distinct capital expenditure (capex) strategies, cash flow dynamics, and competitive frameworks during FY2023–FY2024. Due to retrieval and reporting limitations across the available evidence set, a complete, uniform quantitative comparison of full-year capex and free cash flow across all five entities cannot be established.

* **Tesla** deployed multi-billion-dollar quarterly capex into physical manufacturing and AI infrastructure, generating positive free cash flow in the latter half of 2024 and closing Q4 2024 with $36,563 million in cash and investments [1].
* **Microsoft** prioritized cloud and AI infrastructure expansion in FY2024, achieving annual revenues over $245 billion and operating income over $109 billion alongside 30% growth in Azure and cloud services [2, 3].
* **Alphabet** focused investments on technical infrastructure to support AI and cloud, with projected 2025 capex reaching approximately $75 billion, supported by FY2024 operating income of $112.390 billion [4, 5, 6].
* **NVIDIA** reported FY2024 property and equipment additions of approximately $5.6 billion (about 10% of revenue) and generated approximately $10.8 billion in free cash flow, enabling both capital investments and share repurchases [7].

## Key Findings & Comparative Analysis

### Tesla
Tesla's capital deployment focuses on physical footprint expansion and next-generation technologies. Quarterly capital expenditures were reported at $(2,272) million in Q2 2024, $(3,513) million in Q3 2024, and $(2,780) million in Q4 2024 [1]. Key investment areas include manufacturing capacity (Gigafactories and Megafactory Shanghai), R&D, and AI infrastructure such as Dojo and Full Self-Driving (FSD) development [1]. Supported by net operating cash flows of $3,612 million (Q2), $6,255 million (Q3), and $4,814 million (Q4), Tesla's free cash flow was recorded at $1,340 million (Q2), $2,742 million (Q3), and $2,034 million (Q4) [1]. Tesla closed Q4 2024 with a cash and investment position totaling $36,563 million [1]. Strategically, Tesla directs capital toward scaling vehicle and energy storage production alongside autonomous software to secure long-term market leadership.

### Microsoft
Microsoft's FY2024 capital expenditures supported the expansion of cloud and AI infrastructure, specifically data centers and specialized hardware to scale AI capabilities [2]. Financial performance for FY2024 included annual revenue of over $245 billion (up 16% year-over-year) and operating income of over $109 billion (up 24%) [3]. Management established three primary platforms for the AI era: Copilot, the Copilot stack (combining infrastructure, data, and app services), and Copilot+ PCs [3]. Capital deployment prioritizes seizing technological paradigm shifts to reinvent products, drive consumption-based Azure and cloud services (which grew 30% in FY2024) [2], and bend the productivity curve [8]. Gross margin impacts from scaling AI infrastructure are intended to be offset by strong cloud consumption growth.

### Alphabet (Google)
Alphabet's capital investments target technical infrastructure—including data centers, servers, and networking equipment—to support AI initiatives and cloud computing operations [4]. Management noted that projected capex for 2025 is approximately $75 billion [5]. Alphabet reported FY2024 operating income of $112.390 billion (up from $84,293 million in FY2023) [6] and Q4 2024 net income of $26.5 billion with a diluted EPS of $2.15 [5]. Management emphasizes reinvesting operating cash flows into scaling full-stack AI infrastructure [9]. Its strategy relies on a differentiated full-stack approach across three pillars: leading AI infrastructure, world-class research, and consumer/enterprise products deployed at scale across Search, YouTube, and Google Cloud [9]. Management willingly accepts near-term capital intensity increases to defend and expand its technological moat.

### Meta Platforms
Costs and expenses for full year 2024 were reported at $95,121 million, compared to $88,151 million in 2023, representing an 8% increase [10]. Board disclosures emphasize long-term strategic oversight to lay the foundation for future company success, community value, and shareholder returns [11]. Detailed figures regarding headline capex spending, specific investment breakdowns (such as exact dollar figures for data centers, servers, AI investments, or Reality Labs), free cash flow results, and explicit management commentary on capital allocation were not retrieved within the available evidence window.

### NVIDIA
In its FY2024 Form 10-K, NVIDIA reported capital expenditures (property and equipment additions) of approximately $5.6 billion, representing about 10% of FY2024 revenue [7]. Capacity investments were heavily skewed toward expanding data center infrastructure and advanced manufacturing capacity [7]. NVIDIA generated approximately $10.8 billion of free cash flow in FY2024 [7]. This cash conversion capability absorbed capital expenditures while funding share repurchases, including $477.26 million (1.1 million shares) between November 27 and December 24, 2023, and $540.85 million (3.3 million shares) between December 25, 2023, and January 28, 2024 [7]. Management's strategy emphasizes shifting traditional data centers to accelerated computing and building "AI factories" to produce artificial intelligence [12].
