## Executive Summary
The available evidence regarding capital expenditures and investment strategies for Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Nvidia highlights different disclosures: AMD reports interim property and equipment additions alongside strategic acquisitions, while Nvidia provides broad revenue performance and market-level infrastructure projections, though both companies lack full-year R&D and capex line items from 10-K filings in the current dataset. Key insights include:
* AMD reported purchases of property and equipment of $296 million for the six-month period ended June 29, 2024, compared to $283 million for the six-month period ended July 1, 2023 [1].
* AMD's CEO Lisa Su noted that the full-year outlook for the MI300 increased to $4 billion due to customer commitments [2].
* Nvidia reported fiscal year 2025 revenue of $130.5 billion, representing a 114% year-over-year increase [3].
* Nvidia management cited expectations that big tech data center capital expenditures will reach $1 trillion in 2027, heading toward a $3 trillion to $4 trillion annual spend by 2030 [4].

## AMD Capital Expenditures and Investment Strategies
AMD's investment and capital expenditure disclosures center on interim property additions, corporate liquidity provisions, and inorganic expansion to support artificial intelligence capabilities.

* **Capital Expenditures:** Purchases of property and equipment were $296 million for the six-month period ended June 29, 2024, and $283 million for the six-month period ended July 1, 2023 [1]. Full-year capital expenditure line items covering the entirety of FY2023 and FY2024 are not found [1].
* **Liquidity and Funding:** Forward-looking disclosures indicate that cash, cash equivalents, short-term investments, and available credit facilities are expected to fund operations, capital expenditures, and purchase commitments over the next 12 months and beyond, accounting for emerging AI technologies and regulatory environments [5].
* **Strategic Acquisitions:** AMD highlighted targeted acquisitions, specifically Silo AI Oy and the anticipated acquisition of ZT Group Int'l, Inc., to strengthen its AI capabilities and product portfolio [6].
* **Product and Segment Scaling:** CEO Lisa Su explained that the increase in the full-year outlook for the MI300 from $3.5 billion to $4 billion was driven by greater visibility and commitments from current and new customers [2]. Data center segment revenue grew 80% year-over-year and 2% sequentially to a record $2.3 billion in Q1 2024, driven by AMD Instinct MI300X GPU shipments and server CPU sales [7]. For the full year 2024, the Data Center segment contributed roughly 50% of annual revenue, with a 69% year-over-year increase to a record $3.9 billion in Q4 2024 [8].

## Nvidia Capital Expenditures and Investment Strategies
Nvidia's investment strategy focuses on full-stack computing and networking systems, sustainable infrastructure, and navigating regulatory constraints.

* **Revenue and Financial Scale:** Nvidia reported fiscal year 2025 revenue of $130.5 billion, a 114% increase year-over-year [3]. Additional quarterly figures include Q1 2025 revenue of $44.1 billion with an EPS of $0.96 [9], and Q3 FY2025 revenue of $35.1 billion [10].
* **Data Center Revenue Performance:** Q1 2025 Data Center revenue reached $39 billion, marking a 73% year-on-year increase [9]; Q3 FY2025 Data Center revenue reached $30.8 billion, up 112% year-on-year [10]; and Q3 2024 Data Center revenue was $14.5 billion, up 41% sequentially and 279% year-on-year [11].
* **Ecosystem and Infrastructure Investments:** Technical sessions at GTC 2025 highlighted operational investments in data center energy and water efficiency, alongside engineering sustainable data center infrastructure [12, 13]. Management emphasized an investment strategy centered on delivering full-stack systems—comprising compute, networking, and switches—running the proprietary software stack NVIDIA AI Enterprise [11].
* **Hardware and Software Milestones:** Highlighted milestones include the Hopper Tensor Core architecture, InfiniBand end-to-end networking, the BH200 GPU with HBM3E memory (offering up to 2x inference performance compared to H100), TensorRT-LLM, and Spectrum-X for optimized Ethernet AI workloads [11, 14].
* **Macroeconomic and Regulatory Factors:** Management noted the evaluation of limited options to supply data center compute products compliant with revised U.S. export control rules, warning that losing access to the China AI accelerator market (projected to grow to nearly $50 billion) would have a material adverse impact [9]. Management additionally highlighted industry-wide projections that big tech data center capital expenditures will reach $1 trillion in 2027, maintaining a trajectory toward $3 trillion to $4 trillion in annual spending by 2030 [4].

## Comparative Findings and Market Context
Direct metrics for comparison remain constrained by missing full-year line items for R&D and capital expenditures. While Nvidia reports corporate scale on an annual basis of $130.5 billion in revenue for fiscal year 2025 [3], AMD's disclosures emphasize segment milestones, such as the Data Center segment contributing roughly 50% of annual revenue in 2024 and reaching a record $3.9 billion in Q4 2024 [8]. Both companies frame their investment narratives heavily around the expansion of high-performance AI accelerators (AMD's MI300 series scaling to a $4 billion outlook [2]; Nvidia's Hopper architecture, BH200, and full-stack software integration [11, 14]), while facing external variables such as export controls and data center infrastructure scaling limits.

## Analytical Interpretation and Synthesis
The available data is heavily skewed toward high-level financial performance metrics, quarterly and annual segment revenues, and select interim cash flow statements rather than granular, full-year line-item disclosures for R&D expenses and capital expenditures in SEC filings. AMD's strategy centers on ramping up AI offerings like the MI300 series and executing inorganic expansion via acquisitions such as Silo AI and ZT Group, though specific long-term capex budgets or line-item R&D scaling figures remain absent. Nvidia's strategy focuses on scaling full-stack hardware and software ecosystems (Hopper, BH200, NVIDIA AI Enterprise, TensorRT-LLM), improving data center energy and water efficiency, and monitoring macro and regulatory headwinds like U.S. export controls.
