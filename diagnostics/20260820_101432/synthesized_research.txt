## Direct Answer

A direct comparison of the complete, full-year capital expenditures (CapEx) for Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and NVIDIA is **not possible** using the current primary source disclosures retrieved. Major gaps in primary financial statements exist: full-year property and equipment purchases (direct CapEx) were not found for AMD in FY2023, FY2024, or FY2025, and NVIDIA’s own property and equipment cash flows were completely missing from the retrieved filing snippets for all requested periods. 

However, the available data highlights two fundamentally distinct capital allocation and investment strategies:

1. **AMD's strategy** centers on expanding internal capabilities and geographic reach. AMD raised its first-half property and equipment spend by 39.4% year-over-year in 2023 ($283 million vs. $203 million) [src: https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-23-000139/0000002488-23-000139.pdf] and experienced a massive acceleration in total net cash used in investing activities—surging from $1,101 million in FY2024 to $6,851 million in FY2025 [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000018/amd-20251227.htm]—to position itself for a projected $500 billion data center AI accelerator market by 2028 [src: https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0001193125-25-067185/0001193125-25-067185.pdf].
2. **NVIDIA's strategy** relies on a fabless manufacturing model that shifts heavy infrastructure capital constraints off its balance sheet. Instead of prioritizing its own internal CapEx, NVIDIA captures and directs **customer-side CapEx**. Its strategy is designed to capture the massive capital budgets of top cloud providers and hyperscalers, whose CapEx is projected to reach between $600 billion and $700 billion annually [src: https://www.roic.ai/quote/NVDA:QF/transcripts/2026-year/2-quarter][src: https://www.defenseworld.net/2026/02/26/nvidia-q4-earnings-call-highlights.html]. NVIDIA leverages this by delivering full-stack physical infrastructure ("AI Factories") [src: https://news.futunn.com/en/post/57400285/full-transcript-of-the-nvidia-conference-call-jensen-huang-praised] and expanding into sovereign AI markets, which grew to generate over $30 billion in revenue in FY2026 [src: https://www.defenseworld.net/2026/02/26/nvidia-q4-earnings-call-highlights.html].

## Supporting Evidence

### AMD Capital Expenditures & Investment Strategies

#### 1. CapEx and Cash Outflow Metrics
* **Quarterly/Half-Year CapEx:** For the six months ended July 1, 2023, AMD’s cash outflows for purchases of property and equipment (capital expenditures) were $283 million, representing an increase from the $203 million spent during the six months ended June 25, 2022 [src: https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-23-000139/0000002488-23-000139.pdf].
* **Acquisition Cash Outflows:** During the six months ended July 1, 2023, AMD reported $0 in cash outflows for the acquisition of Pensando, net of cash acquired, compared to $1,558 million spent during the six months ended June 25, 2022 [src: https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-23-000139/0000002488-23-000139.pdf].
* **Net Investing Cash Flows:** AMD's net cash used in investing activities of continuing operations was $1,101 million for the fiscal year ended December 28, 2024, which surged to $6,851 million for the fiscal year ended December 27, 2025 [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000018/amd-20251227.htm]. *(Note: Direct breakouts of full-year property and equipment purchases for FY24 and FY25 were not retrieved).*

#### 2. Strategic Targets and Capacity Investments
* **Geographic R&D Expansion:** In October 2023, AMD announced plans to invest approximately $400 million over the next five years to expand its research, development, and engineering operations in India, intending to add approximately 3,000 new engineering roles by the end of 2028 [src: https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2023-10-31-amd-reports-third-quarter-2023-financial-results.html].
* **AI TAM Expectations:** In its 2024 Annual Report, management projected that the total addressable market (TAM) for AI accelerators in the data center is expected to grow by more than 60% annually, reaching $500 billion by 2028 [src: https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0001193125-25-067185/0001193125-25-067185.pdf].

### NVIDIA Capital Expenditures & Investment Strategies

#### 1. Capital Spent through Customer Ecosystems ("AI Factories")
* **Hyperscaler CapEx Growth:** In Q2 FY2026, NVIDIA highlighted that cloud and enterprise CapEx was on track to reach $600 billion for data center infrastructure and compute within the calendar year, representing a near doubling over a two-year period [src: https://www.roic.ai/quote/NVDA:QF/transcripts/2026-year/2-quarter][src: https://www.earningscall.ai/stock/transcript/NVDA-2026-Q2].
* **Top-Five Cloud Providers:** In Q4 FY2026, CFO Colette Kress noted that analyst expectations for 2026 CapEx among the top five cloud providers and hyperscalers (who account for just over 50% of NVIDIA's data center revenue) had increased by nearly $120 billion since the beginning of the year, approaching $700 billion [src: https://www.defenseworld.net/2026/02/26/nvidia-q4-earnings-call-highlights.html].
* **Sovereign AI Infrastructure:** Sovereign nations funding domestic AI infrastructure represent a key market-driving investment. NVIDIA’s sovereign AI business tripled year-over-year in FY2026, generating over $30 billion in revenue, led by projects in Canada, France, the Netherlands, Singapore, and the United Kingdom [src: https://www.defenseworld.net/2026/02/26/nvidia-q4-earnings-call-highlights.html].

#### 2. Strategic and Venture Capital Spending
* **NVentures Investments:** NVIDIA allocates capital through its venture capital arm, NVentures, targeting equity investments in physical AI and energy grid optimizations for AI factories (including startups Skild AI and Emerald AI), though specific transaction values are undisclosed [src: https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/startups/nventures].

#### 3. Technology Scaling and AI Factory Roadmap
* **Efficiency Scaling:** In Q2 FY2026, management detailed that its upcoming GV300 NDL72 AI factories deliver a 10x improvement in token-per-watt energy efficiency compared to the Hopper generation [src: https://www.roic.ai/quote/NVDA:QF/transcripts/2026-year/2-quarter][src: https://www.earningscall.ai/stock/transcript/NVDA-2026-Q2].
* **Production Volume:** Blackwell production reached a pace of 72,000 GPUs shipped weekly to meet inference demand, notably supporting Microsoft's processing of 100 trillion tokens in a single quarter [src: https://news.futunn.com/en/post/57400285/full-transcript-of-the-nvidia-conference-call-jensen-huang-praised].
* **Infrastructure Pipeline:** During Q4 FY2026, NVIDIA supported nearly 100 AI factories under construction, representing a year-over-year doubling of factory counts and a doubling of average GPUs deployed per factory [src: https://news.futunn.com/en/post/57400285/full-transcript-of-the-nvidia-conference-call-jensen-huang-praised].
* **Long-Term Scaling Vision:** Management projects a $3 trillion to $4 trillion global AI infrastructure opportunity by the end of the decade, shifting from Hopper clusters to hundreds of thousands of Blackwell GPUs in 100-megawatt facilities, eventually scaling to multi-site "super-factories" utilizing millions of Rubin GPUs [src: https://www.roic.ai/quote/NVDA:QF/transcripts/2026-year/2-quarter][src: https://www.earningscall.ai/stock/transcript/NVDA-2026-Q2].

## Synthesis and Strategic Interpretation

The strategic frameworks of AMD and NVIDIA present a stark contrast in semiconductor asset management and capital intensity:

* **Internal Infrastructure vs. Ecosystem Enablement:** AMD relies heavily on physical asset expansion and R&D footprint growth to scale. Even with missing full-year direct CapEx numbers, the rapid rise of AMD's total net investing cash outflows from $1,101 million (FY24) to $6,851 million (FY25) [src: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000018/amd-20251227.htm] represents an aggressive expansion phase. AMD is positioning itself to capture a significant portion of the $500 billion data center accelerator market by securing regional engineering talent (such as its $400 million investment in India) [src: https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2023-10-31-amd-reports-third-quarter-2023-financial-results.html] and building out its portfolio.
* **Ecosystem-funded Asset-Light Strategy:** NVIDIA employs a fabless, ecosystem-funded model. Its direct internal CapEx is kept highly constrained. Instead, NVIDIA directs and captures the surging CapEx budgets of external hyperscalers, cloud builders, and sovereign nations. By orchestrating a $600 billion to $700 billion annual industry capital spend pool [src: https://www.roic.ai/quote/NVDA:QF/transcripts/2026-year/2-quarter][src: https://www.defenseworld.net/2026/02/26/nvidia-q4-earnings-call-highlights.html], NVIDIA can return billions of dollars to shareholders (such as $37.0B returned YTD in Q3 FY2026) [src: https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2025/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2026] and sustain high-margin, massive revenue growth without clogging its balance sheet with high-risk fixed assets.
