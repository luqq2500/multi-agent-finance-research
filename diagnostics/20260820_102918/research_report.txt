## Executive Summary
The available evidence reveals distinct approaches between NVIDIA and AMD regarding AI infrastructure spending and capital allocation strategies. NVIDIA emphasizes large-scale macroeconomic forecasting and hyper-growth market sizing linked to its Blackwell and Rubin architectures [1], whereas AMD reports concrete multi-year financial results alongside targeted strategic divestments and R&D scaling for its Instinct MI300 and MI350 accelerator series [3, 4, 5, 6]. Granular, itemized capital expenditure (PP&E) and exact R&D dollar figures from regulatory 10-K filings for both companies were not returned in the filing snippets.

* NVIDIA management highlights a projected $3 trillion to $4 trillion global AI infrastructure opportunity over the next five years, driven by hyperscaler demand and accelerated AI computing [1, 2].
* NVIDIA's data center revenue reached roughly $41 billion for the quarter ending July 27, with top customers accounting for about 40% of segment revenue [2].
* AMD reported FY 2024 revenue of $25.8 billion with a Non-GAAP operating income of $6.1 billion, followed by FY 2025 revenue of $34.6 billion and a Non-GAAP operating income of $3.7 billion [3, 5].
* AMD announced an agreement to divest the ZT Systems data center infrastructure manufacturing business to Sanmina for $3 billion (inclusive of a contingent payment of up to $450 million) while retaining rack-scale AI design expertise [7].

## NVIDIA Strategy and Market Outlook
NVIDIA's strategy centers on forecasting macro-level demand and aligning its product generations with massive data center investments by cloud providers. Management projects that global data center capital expenditures will rise toward $1 trillion by 2027 to support accelerated computing transitions that require 1,000 times higher computation power [2, 8, 10]. Furthermore, NVIDIA forecasts a $3 trillion to $4 trillion total AI infrastructure market over the next five years, anchored by the introduction of the Blackwell and Rubin architectures [1]. Hyperscalers continue to drive significant top-line figures, with NVIDIA's data center revenue reaching approximately $41 billion for the quarter ending July 27, during which top customers represented roughly 40% of the segment's revenue [2].

## AMD Financial Performance and Capital Allocation
AMD's financial and capital allocation strategy balances revenue growth with targeted investments in product engineering and operating expenses. Operating expenses increased significantly to support R&D and go-to-market strategies tailored for data center and AI opportunities [6].

* **FY 2024 Revenue:** $25.8 billion [3]
* **FY 2024 Non-GAAP Operating Income:** $6.1 billion [3]
* **FY 2025 Revenue:** $34.6 billion [5]
* **FY 2025 Non-GAAP Operating Income:** $3.7 billion [5]

To support data center expansion, AMD pursued a steep ramp of the AMD Instinct MI350 series accelerators following prior deployment of the MI300 series [6]. Concurrently, AMD executed a major capital allocation shift by agreeing to divest the ZT Systems manufacturing business to Sanmina for $3 billion (including a contingent payment of up to $450 million) [7]. Under this agreement, AMD retains ZT Systems' rack-scale AI solutions design and customer enablement expertise [7].

## Analytical Interpretation and Synthesis
Based on the supplied evidence, the two companies exhibit divergent structural frameworks in managing their AI-driven expansion:

* **NVIDIA** leans heavily into macro-level demand forecasting, leveraging upcoming product lifecycles like Blackwell and Rubin to capture multi-trillion-dollar infrastructure buildouts by hyperscalers [1].
* **AMD** implements a capital-light manufacturing posture by divesting heavy physical factory assets (such as the ZT Systems manufacturing unit to Sanmina) while preserving internal rack-scale design talent [7, 9]. This strategy minimizes direct manufacturing capital expenditures on heavy factory assets while scaling R&D for accelerators such as the Instinct MI300 and MI350 series [6, 9].
