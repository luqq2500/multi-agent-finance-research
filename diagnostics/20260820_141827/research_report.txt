# Macroeconomic and Market Data Across Cycle Periods

## Executive Summary
The available evidence provides macroeconomic and market metrics across selected historical periods (2007–2009, 2020, and 2022–2023), but lacks the data required to apply Ray Dalio's economic cycles framework to S&P 500 regime cycles. As noted in the source synthesis, the empirical records present factual data points without applying Dalio's framework or synthesizing forward-looking investment rules.

* Year-over-year CPI inflation fluctuated across the examined periods, moving from 2.8% in 2007 and 3.8% in 2008 to a deflationary rate of approximately –0.4% in 2009 [1], an estimated 1.4% in 2020 [1], and surging to roughly 6.5% in 2022 [1].
* The federal funds target rate shifted between a 0%–0.25% floor during the 2008–2009 and 2020 downturns and a tightening range of 5.25%–5.50% by December 2023 [2].
* Credit conditions saw extreme divergence, with the ICE BofA US High Yield Index Option-Adjusted Spread reaching a record low of 2.41% in June 2007 before surging to a record high of 21.82% in December 2008 [3].
* Both the S&P 500 and long-term Treasuries lost more than 20% from peak to valley in 2022, marking an unusual year of negative returns [4, 5].

## Key Findings & Comparative Analysis

### Macroeconomic Indicators
* **CPI / PCE Inflation Rates:** In 2007 and 2008, year-over-year CPI inflation was approximately 2.8% and 3.8% respectively, before dropping to approximately –0.4% in 2009 [1]. In 2020, the annual CPI increase was estimated around 1.4% [1]. Inflation surged to roughly 6.5% in 2022, and by August 2023, the BLS reported a year-over-year All-Items CPI rise of 3.7% [1, 6].
* **Federal Reserve Policy Rates:** The federal funds target rate peaked at 5.25% in 2007, dropped to 0%–0.25% by late 2008, and remained there through 2009 [2]. The target rate was similarly lowered to 0%–0.25% in March 2020 and stayed at that floor throughout the year [2]. Monetary tightening subsequently brought the target rate to 4.25%–4.50% by December 2022, 4.75%–5.00% by mid-2023, and 5.25%–5.50% by December 2023 [2].
* **Real GDP Growth and Unemployment Rates:** Employment fell more rapidly during the December 2007 – June 2009 recession than in prior recessions [7]. The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic caused real GDP to contract at annualized rates of 5.1% in Q1 2020 and 31.2% in Q2 2020 [8]. In Q1 2022, real GDP dropped 1.4% while remaining 3.6% higher than a year earlier [8]. In 2023, real GDP registered a 2.1% gain in Q2 and accelerated to an annualized rate of 4.9% in Q3 [9]. The unemployment rate stood at 3.6% at the end of 2022 and was projected to rise to 5.1% by the end of 2023 [10].
* **Credit Condition Metrics:** The ICE BofA US High Yield Index Option-Adjusted Spread reached a record low of 2.41% in June 2007 and surged to a record high of 21.82% in December 2008 [3].

### S&P 500 Market Valuations, Performance, and Volatility
* **2007–2009 Transitions:** Implied volatility on S&P 500 options surged in 2007, with the VIX entering a pattern of higher highs and higher lows prior to the market peak [11]. Price-to-earnings, price-to-book, and price-to-sales ratios reached record highs prior to the financial crisis [11]. Following the March 2009 bottom, S&P 500 market capitalization grew from 41% of U.S. GDP to 212% [11].
* **2022 Performance:** The S&P 500 and long-term Treasuries both lost more than 20% from peak to valley [4, 5].
* **Post-2022 / 2023 Performance:** Following the 2022 inflation scare, the S&P 500 achieved three consecutive years of 15%-plus total returns [4].

## Evidence Gaps & Data Limitations
The available evidence is insufficient to determine specific monthly Treasury yield levels (such as 2-year and 10-year constant maturity rates) for each individual month in 2007–2009, 2020, and 2022–2023. Additionally, specific quantitative S&P 500 annual returns, daily trading volumes, and volatility metrics for the 2007–2009 macroeconomic transition are missing, alongside exact annual S&P 500 trading volumes and specific volatility metrics or VIX levels for 2020, 2022, and 2023. Detailed analyst consensus measures and specific published market valuation ratios (other than qualitative mentions of P/E, P/B, P/S records and CAPE ratios) for 2020 and 2022–2023 were also not found.

## Analytical Interpretation & Synthesis
The empirical records present factual data points but do not apply Ray Dalio's economic cycles framework or synthesize forward-looking investment rules. To fully map historical metrics onto Dalio’s framework (which evaluates cycles through debt deleveragings, productivity, short-term debt cycles/business cycles, and long-term debt cycles), additional data points are required. Specifically, comprehensive monthly Treasury yield curves, explicit credit growth aggregates, private debt-to-income ratios, and granular daily volatility and volume metrics across 2020 and 2022–2023 are missing. Without these inputs, any attempt to classify specific structural shifts into distinct frameworks would rely on unverified assumptions rather than exhaustive data.
