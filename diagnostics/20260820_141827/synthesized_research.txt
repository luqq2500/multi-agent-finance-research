### Macroeconomic and Market Data Across Cycle Periods

#### 1. Macroeconomic Indicators

*   **CPI / PCE Inflation Rates:**
    *   **2007–2009:** Year-over-year Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation was approximately 2.8% in 2007 and 3.8% in 2008, dropping to a deflationary rate of approximately –0.4% in 2009 [src: https://www.investopedia.com/inflation-rate-by-year-7253832].
    *   **2020:** The annual CPI increase was estimated around 1.4% [src: https://www.investopedia.com/inflation-rate-by-year-7253832].
    *   **2022–2023:** Year-over-year inflation surged to roughly 6.5% in 2022 [src: https://www.investopedia.com/inflation-rate-by-year-7253832]. By August 2023, the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) reported a year-over-year All-Items CPI rise of 3.7% [src: https://www.forbes.com/sites/andrewdepietro/2023/09/13/analysis-of-consumer-price-index-cpi-latest-release].

*   **Federal Reserve Policy Rates:**
    *   **2007–2009:** The federal funds target rate peaked at 5.25% in 2007, dropped to a 0%–0.25% range by late 2008, and remained there through 2009 [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_funds_rate].
    *   **2020:** The target rate was lowered to 0%–0.25% in March 2020 and stayed at that floor throughout the year [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_funds_rate].
    *   **2022–2023:** Monetary tightening brought the target rate to 4.25%–4.50% by December 2022, 4.75%–5.00% by mid-2023, and 5.25%–5.50% by December 2023 [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_funds_rate].

*   **Real GDP Growth and Unemployment Rates:**
    *   **2007–2009:** Employment fell more rapidly during the December 2007 – June 2009 recession than in prior recessions [src: https://www.bls.gov/spotlight/2012/recession].
    *   **2020:** The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic caused real GDP to contract at an annualized rate of 5.1% in Q1 2020 and 31.2% in Q2 2020 [src: https://www.cbpp.org/research/economy/tracking-the-post-great-recession-economy].
    *   **2022–2023:** In Q1 2022, real GDP dropped 1.4% (while remaining 3.6% higher than a year earlier) [src: https://www.cbpp.org/research/economy/tracking-the-post-great-recession-economy]. In 2023, real GDP registered a 2.1% gain in Q2 and accelerated to an annualized rate of 4.9% in Q3 [src: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1850]. The unemployment rate stood at 3.6% at the end of 2022 and was projected to rise to 5.1% by the end of 2023 [src: https://www.cbo.gov/publication/58957].

*   **Credit Condition Metrics:**
    *   The ICE BofA US High Yield Index Option-Adjusted Spread (OAS) reached a record low of 2.41% in June 2007 [src: https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/bofa-merrill-lynch-us-high-yield-option-adjusted-spread-fed-data.html].
    *   During the 2007–2009 financial crisis, the OAS surged to a record high of 21.82% in December 2008 [src: https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/bofa-merrill-lynch-us-high-yield-option-adjusted-spread-fed-data.html].

#### 2. S&P 500 Market Valuations, Performance, and Volatility

*   **2007–2009 Transitions:** Implied volatility on S&P 500 options surged in 2007, with the VIX entering a pattern of higher highs and higher lows prior to the market peak [src: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/equities-spot-market-peak-rally-060000830.html]. Prior to the financial crisis, price-to-earnings, price-to-book, and price-to-sales ratios reached record highs [src: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/equities-spot-market-peak-rally-060000830.html]. Following the March 2009 bottom, the S&P 500 market capitalization grew from 41% of U.S. GDP to 212% [src: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/equities-spot-market-peak-rally-060000830.html].
*   **2022 Performance:** The S&P 500 and long-term Treasuries both lost more than 20% from peak to valley, marking an unusual year of negative returns [src: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/turning-point-coming-p-500-110500151.html, src: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/2022-was-an-unusual-year-for-the-stock-market-%F0%9F%93%89-134223630.html].
*   **Post-2022 / 2023 Performance:** Following the 2022 inflation scare, the S&P 500 achieved three consecutive years of 15%-plus total returns [src: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/turning-point-coming-p-500-110500151.html].
